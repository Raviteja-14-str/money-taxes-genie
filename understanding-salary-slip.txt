TITLE: Understanding Your Salary Slip

Most people never really read their salary slip beyond the final "in-hand" number. But it's one of the most useful documents you get every month, and understanding it helps you catch errors, plan taxes, and know exactly what you're being paid for.

BASIC PAY is the core of your salary, usually 40-50% of your total CTC (Cost to Company). Almost every other component — PF, gratuity, HRA — is calculated as a percentage of Basic. A higher Basic means higher PF contribution (good for retirement savings) but also slightly lower take-home pay today, since more gets deducted.

HRA (House Rent Allowance) is paid to help cover rent. It's partially or fully tax-exempt if you actually pay rent and can show proof (usually rent receipts, and a landlord PAN if annual rent exceeds a threshold). If you own your home and don't pay rent, HRA becomes fully taxable.

SPECIAL ALLOWANCE is a flexible, fully taxable component companies use to balance the total CTC number after fixing Basic and HRA. There's no special tax treatment here — it's taxed like regular salary.

PROVIDENT FUND (PF) is a mandatory retirement savings deduction. Both you and your employer typically contribute 12% of your Basic pay to your PF account. Your contribution is deducted from your salary; your employer's contribution is added on top (it's part of your CTC but not your take-home pay). This money earns interest and is meant to be withdrawn at retirement, though partial withdrawal is allowed under specific conditions.

PROFESSIONAL TAX is a small state-level tax (varies by state, usually a few hundred rupees a month) deducted directly from your salary. It's not related to income tax — it's a separate state government levy.

TDS (Tax Deducted at Source) is your employer estimating your annual tax liability and deducting a portion every month, so you're not hit with a huge tax bill at year-end. It's an advance payment of your income tax, not an extra tax.

GROSS SALARY is your total earnings before any deductions. NET SALARY (or "in-hand" salary) is what actually lands in your bank account after PF, professional tax, TDS, and any other deductions are subtracted from Gross.

A quick way to read any slip: start from CTC (what the company spends on you) → subtract employer's PF and other employer contributions to get Gross Salary → subtract PF (your share), professional tax, and TDS to get Net Salary (your take-home).
