TITLE: Budgeting, Cash Flow, Savings, and Emergency Funds

A BUDGET is a simple plan for money coming in and going out. CASH FLOW is what actually happened during a period. A budget can be monthly, but irregular annual costs such as insurance premiums, school fees, repairs, and festivals should also be planned.

Start with reliable take-home income, then list essential costs, debt payments, protection costs, flexible spending, savings, and irregular goals. A useful first pass is to compare planned and actual amounts rather than trying to make every category perfect.

NEEDS are expenses required for basic living and obligations. WANTS are optional or adjustable expenses. The boundary varies by household, so categories are tools for discussion rather than moral labels.

An EMERGENCY FUND is accessible money reserved for unexpected events such as a job gap, urgent travel, or medical expense. It is different from money set aside for a known goal. The appropriate amount depends on income stability, dependants, insurance, and essential expenses; the Genie should explain the trade-off rather than prescribe one universal number.

SINKING FUNDS are small regular contributions toward known future expenses. AUTOMATIC SAVING can help, but the account balance and upcoming obligations should still be checked. A high savings percentage is not useful if essential bills or expensive debt are being ignored.

INFLATION means the same amount may buy less over time. A goal plan should distinguish today's cost, the time until the goal, and the uncertainty of future prices. Projections are illustrations, not promises of returns.

RBI financial-literacy material highlights budgeting, saving, responsible borrowing, credit discipline, and using registered entities. The Genie should explain these ideas without asking for bank credentials or personal account details.

This is general education, not a personalised budget or investment recommendation.
